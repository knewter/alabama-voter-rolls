01 — Finding

The numbers reproduce.

What the match does not show. This is a registration count compared with a survey estimate. It is not proof of illegal votes. Inactive registrants may vote after reidentification, and an October 2026 roll is being set against an ACS 2020–2024 estimate.

A public post on November 10, 2026 said Alabama’s official voter roll was 100.2% of the Census figure for adult citizens, that 32 of 67 counties were over 100%, that 28 stayed over after the margin of error, and that the roll includes 462,824 inactive registrations. Those figures match the Secretary of State’s October 2026 worksheet and the Census Bureau’s 2020–2024 citizen voting-age population (CVAP) special tabulation.

03 — How to read this

Two different kinds of numbers.

The numerator is a roll

It is a count of people on Alabama’s official registration list in October 2026. Active and inactive together are the official total: 3,390,357 + 462,824 = 3,853,181. Inactive registrants remain on that list. They may vote after reidentification.

The denominator is an estimate

CVAP here is the Census special tabulation of U.S. citizens age 18 and older, from the 2020–2024 American Community Survey. The state estimate is 3,845,696, plus or minus 3,237. That margin is an approximate 90% confidence interval, not a 95% interval. CVAP does not remove disqualifications under Alabama law, so it is not a count of people currently allowed to vote.

The dates do not match

The roll is an October 2026 snapshot. The estimate summarizes surveys from 2020 through 2024 and was published January 30, 2026. In a fast-growing county, the roll can include adult citizens the survey window has not fully picked up. A person can also be counted in one county under Census residency rules and be registered in another.

Over 100% is a gap between a list and an estimate

It means the roll is larger than the estimate. It is not proof of illegal votes. This site does not count ballots and does not identify any person. A rate above the upper bound means the gap is larger than the published margin of error. That is still a comparison of a list to a survey.

The four counties inside the margin

Thirty-two counties are above the estimate. Twenty-eight of those are also above the upper bound. The other four are above the estimate only: Lawrence (100.6%, 144 over, margin ±167), Escambia (100.3%, 94 over, margin ±105), Covington (100.3%, 80 over, margin ±84), and Chambers (100.1%, 32 over, margin ±107).

Active registrations only

Leaving inactive registrations out of the rate, the state figure is 88.16% (3,390,357 ÷ 3,845,696), which is 455,339 below the estimate. Half-up to one decimal, that rate is 88.2%. Three counties stay above 100% on active registrations alone: Lowndes 105.1%, Perry 104.3%, and Washington 100.7%. Lowndes and Perry also clear the upper bound. Washington does not: 12,164 active registrations, against an upper bound of 12,172.

    05 — Method

    The arithmetic, in one place.

    Registrations in the claim’s comparison are active plus inactive. The sensitivity check uses active alone. The estimate is cvap_est. The margin of error is cvap_moe.

    percent = 100 × registrations ÷ cvap_estpercent equals 100 times registrations divided by the CVAP estimate over = registrations − cvap_estover equals registrations minus the CVAP estimate above the estimate when registrations > cvap_estabove the estimate when registrations are greater than the CVAP estimate above the upper bound when registrations > cvap_est + cvap_moeabove the upper bound when registrations are greater than the CVAP estimate plus the margin of error

    One-decimal percentages use half-up rounding. A second decimal of 5 rounds away from zero. 100.15 would print as 100.2, and 100.14 would print as 100.1. The county file stores both the longer ratio and the one-decimal result.

    Worked statewide example, using the state CVAP row rather than the sum of the counties: 3,853,181 ÷ 3,845,696 × 100 = 100.194633%, which half-up rounds to 100.2%. The difference is 3,853,181 − 3,845,696 = 7,485. The upper bound is 3,845,696 + 3,237 = 3,848,933. The official total is 4,248 above that bound, so the same rule that flags a county also flags the state total.

    Active only: 3,390,357 ÷ 3,845,696 × 100 = 88.159777%, printed here as 88.16%. Half-up to one decimal is 88.2%. The gap is 3,390,357 − 3,845,696 = −455,339.

    Inactive registrations are 462,824 ÷ 3,845,696 of the estimate, 12.03 percentage points. Adding them to the active rate is what moves the state from 88.16% to 100.2%.

    The 67 county estimates sum to 3,845,715, which is 19 higher than the state estimate of 3,845,696. Census rounds each published estimate, so the counties do not have to add up to the state. The statewide rate uses the state row.

    The October worksheet lists each county plus a total. Column B is the official total, column L is active, and column V is inactive. The sheet’s total equals column L plus column V in every row used here. Two worksheet names differ from the Census names: ST_CLAIR is St. Clair, and DEKALB is DeKalb.

    The reproduction notes that ACS table B29001, same 2020–2024 vintage, is within ±4 people of this special tabulation at the county level. The over counts match the special tabulation, not B29001.
